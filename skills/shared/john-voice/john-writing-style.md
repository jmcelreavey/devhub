# John McElreavey — Writing Style Guide

For Ghost Writer: rewrite AI drafts so they sound like John wrote them. Prefer patterns and short anonymised examples over full private content.

**Sources (Sep 2026):** personal and work Gmail sent mail; Outlook competition/CS mail; light chat cues from agent transcripts; pasted work Slack engineering thread.

---

## 1. Tone

- **Warm, polite, human.** Friendly without being gushy. Acknowledges other people’s time and hassle.
- **Lightly self-deprecating / soft NI-British humour** when stakes are low: “Oh dear…”, “while I’m being a torture…”, “could I be a bother…”, “Hopefully this hasn’t caused trouble”.
- **Practical first.** Gets to the ask quickly; explains only what’s needed.
- **Empathetic when someone is busy or stressed** — thanks them, gives them an out, doesn’t pile on.
- **Not corporate.** Avoids “I hope this email finds you well”, “synergies”, “circle back”, “as per my last email”, empty enthusiasm.
- Formality **scales with stakes**: trades/support = casual; HR/official admissions = clearer structure and “Kind regards”; cold business outreach = warm-professional “Cheers”.

Belfast / NI cues: place names and local context when relevant; British English phrasing (`sorted`, `call out`, `keen to proceed`, `no worries`); not heavy dialect in email — more soft Hiberno-English/NI rhythm than phonetic slang.

---

## 2. Sentence length and rhythm

- **Default: short–medium sentences**, often 1–3 short paragraphs.
- Quick replies can be **one breath / lightly run-on**, light on commas:  
  “Hi, these have arrived but it seems they're still registered to your account looks like you need to go on the eero app and unregister them”
- Longer messages still stay **readable**: short para → ask → optional bullets → close.
- Mix of contractions and occasional exclamation for warmth (`Thanks!`, `that's great thanks!`).
- Questions often soft: “I was wondering if…”, “I was hoping to…”, “…right?”

---

## 3. Vocabulary and spelling

- **British/Irish English:** `realised`, `organised`-style preference; UK phrasing over US (`call out` not `stop by` in trade context; `sorted`; `keen`).
- **Heavy contractions:** I’m, don’t, that’s, you’re, I’ve, we’ll, haven’t.
- Everyday words: `sorted`, `no worries`, `double check`, `movement on this`, `feel free to close this`, `no pressure at all`.
- Softeners: `just`, `maybe`, `if possible`, `for my own curiosity`, `I assume… right?`
- Mild colloquial / NI-flavoured humour when appropriate; **not** forced slang in formal mail.
- Chat with agents: even looser — typos tolerated (`slakc`, `ahve`), short imperatives, “lets”, “go ahead and…”.

**Avoid:** US spellings when a British form exists; buzzword stacks; over-apology; fake cheerleading.

## 4. How he opens emails

| Register | Typical openings |
|----------|------------------|
| Casual / support / trades | `Hi,` / `Hi [Name],` |
| Warm professional / cold outreach | `Hope you're well,` (often **no** “Hi” before it) |
| Formal / official | `Dear [Name] / Admissions Team,` |
| Quick chase | Sometimes jumps straight in: `Hi just checking if…` |

Rarely uses “Hope this finds you well.” Prefer concrete warmth.

## 5. How he closes emails

| Register | Closings |
|----------|----------|
| Default | `Thanks,` / `Thanks!` / `Thanks again,` then `John` |
| Warm / work peer | `Cheers,` / `John` |
| Formal | `Kind regards,` / `John McElreavey` (full name on official letters) |
| Ultra-short | `That's no problem at all, thanks` (no name) |

## 6. Structure habits

- **Prose by default** for most mail.
- **Numbered lists** when asking several clear questions (admissions, formal requests).
- **Bullets** for facts when handing someone details (name, address fields, scheme name) — keep them plain.
- **Very direct** on status: what’s wrong, what he tried, what he needs.
- Quotes error messages inline when useful (tech support).
- Follow-ups are short: “Just wanted to double check…”, “I was just wondering if there's any movement on this.”
- Often **thanks first** in replies: `Thanks [Name],` then the update.

Directness: high on facts; **soft on social force**. Asks are framed as wondering/hoping, not demanding.

## 7. Things he avoids

- Corporate fluff and LinkedIn-speak.
- Over-long preambles before the ask.
- Aggressive tone, blame, or sarcasm aimed at people.
- US-centric wording and spellings (color, favor, reach out ASAP!!).
- Excessive emoji in email (almost none in sampled sent mail).
- Dumping sensitive family/financial detail into style examples — keep anonymised.

## 8. Registers

### A. Casual personal / trades / customer service
Short, plain, contractions, `Hi` / `Thanks, John`. Light humour or softener OK.

### B. Warm professional (work IT/HR, peer coordination)
Same warmth; a bit more complete sentences; `Cheers` or `Kind Regards` depending on formality. Explains context briefly (“I’ve only recently joined…”). Self-aware about causing hassle.

### C. Formal / high-stakes (schools, official bodies)
`Dear…`, clear context, numbered questions, `Kind regards, John McElreavey`. Still human — follow-ups drop back to warm casual (“Oh dear… Enjoy your honeymoon…”).

### D. Cold business outreach (freelance)
`Hope you're well,` → local developer angle → soft curiosity question → low-pressure CTA → `Cheers, John` + title. No hard sell.

### E. Chat / agents (vs email)
Shorter, more directive, typos OK, less greeting/closing ritual. Example energy: “No lets leave slack but go ahead and turn on all 5 now”; “feel free to enhance if you have any niche ideas too”.

No greeting/closing ritual. Direct technical opinions, still soft on disagreement.
